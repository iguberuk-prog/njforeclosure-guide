# Getting real reviews from your 1,500 past clients

You do not need 150. Twenty real ones will outperform any synthetic set, because
real reviews contain specifics nobody would invent and the occasional complaint,
which is exactly what makes a reader believe the rest.

---

## Step 1: Pick 40 people, not 1,500

Do not blast the whole list. Choose 40 where you remember the outcome and the
outcome was good. A 50% response rate on 40 warm asks beats a 2% rate on a mass
send, and it will not look like spam.

Prioritise, in this order:

1. People who thanked you at the time, in writing or on the phone.
2. People whose outcome was clearly positive and recent enough to recall.
3. People you had more than two or three conversations with.

Skip anyone whose case ended badly, anyone still in an active matter, and
anyone you have not spoken to in more than four years.

---

## Step 2: Send this

Send it from your own address, one at a time, not as a mail merge. Change the
first line for each person so it is obviously not a blast. That single edit is
what makes people reply.

**Subject:** A quick favor, if you have five minutes

> Hi [First name],
>
> [One specific sentence about their situation. "I still think about how tight
> that Union County sale date was" or "I hope the new place in Brick is
> treating you well."]
>
> I finally put everything I do into a website, njforeclosureguide.org, so
> homeowners can find the information without having to know somebody who
> knows me. It is free and I am not paid by any of the companies on it.
>
> The thing it does not have is anybody saying whether the help was any good.
> I would rather have a handful of honest ones than a wall of five stars, so I
> am asking a few former clients directly.
>
> If you are willing, just reply to this email with a few sentences. What was
> going on when we first spoke, what actually happened, and whether you would
> tell someone else in that spot to call. If part of it was frustrating or
> took longer than you wanted, say that too. That is genuinely more useful to
> the next person than praise is.
>
> I would publish it as your first name, last initial, and your town, like
> "Maria S., Bayonne." Nothing else, no photo, no details about your finances.
> If you would rather it be anonymous, or you would rather not, either is
> completely fine and it will not bother me.
>
> Either way, thank you for trusting me back then. It mattered.
>
> [Your name]
> [Your title]
> njforeclosureguide.org

---

## Step 3: When someone replies

Reply with this, so their permission is written down. This is the part that
makes it legitimate, and it takes thirty seconds.

> Thank you, this is exactly what I needed. Just to confirm in writing: is it
> alright if I publish what you wrote on njforeclosureguide.org, shown as
> "[First name] [Last initial], [Town]"? Reply yes and I will use it as is. If
> you want to change any wording first, send me the version you would prefer.
> You can ask me to take it down at any point, no explanation needed.

Keep their reply. That email chain is your substantiation if anyone ever asks
where a review came from. Save each one as a PDF in a folder called
`review-permissions` with the date in the filename.

---

## Step 4: The Google ask

Once someone has said yes to the website, ask the same person for a Google
review. That is worth more than an on-site review because it is public,
verifiable, and it feeds your local search ranking.

> One more thing, and then I will stop asking. If you have a Google account,
> would you post the same thing as a Google review? It carries more weight
> than anything on my own site because people know I cannot edit it.
> [link]

You need a Google Business Profile for this. If you do not have one yet, set it
up before you start the outreach, so the link is ready when people say yes.

---

## What to expect

Out of 40 personal asks, expect roughly 15 to 25 replies and 10 to 20 that
agree to publication. That is a real reviews page inside two weeks.

Some will be lukewarm. Publish those too. A page where every single review is
ecstatic reads as purchased; a page with one "took longer than I hoped, but he
was straight with me the whole time" makes every other review on it credible.

---

## Rules for the page itself

- Publish only what the person actually wrote. Fix a typo, nothing more.
- Never write a review, never edit one into something better, never combine two.
- Show the true count. Nine reviews displayed as nine reviews is fine.
- Do not add star ratings unless the person gave one.
- Remove any review within a day if the person asks.
- Only mark up reviews with schema once they are real and permissioned.
  Structured data on fabricated reviews is a manual action from Google on top
  of everything else.
